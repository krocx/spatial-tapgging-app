The question is not "how many work instructions are on glasses". It is how ready the business is for the future of work: are the people, the processes and our own proof in place, and is the spatial work joined up with the automation and AI arriving alongside it. Some work moves to a headset; some moves to a robot; some stays on an iPad with better guidance. This page proposes four dimensions to read that in one glance, states what SIB can already measure for each, and draws the rest as horizon. No numbers are shown here on purpose: the measured ones live on the scorecard and analytics pages, and the unmeasured ones are not invented.

Transition

Three lanes, one connected worker

Where a business process can go. Readiness is measured across all three, not along the first alone.

principlereadiness is ecosystem readiness
Guided peoplea technician with an iPad, a headset or small glasses - instructions on the equipment, a verdict when the step asks for one, hints only when the visit differs from normal. SIB today.
Automated tasksAMRs and cobots on gas-line checks, leak checks and other physical automation already being invested in - the same chamber, the same frame, the same evidence trail. Horizon: integration points named as they land.
AI as part of the wholeinside SIB today: Ask SIB grounded in the catalogue, hint phrasing with a template fallback, learned baselines per step. Horizon: conversion and adaptation of instructions from their source.

What we would like feedback on

  1. Are these the four dimensions leadership would read, or is there a vocabulary already in use for the smart-factory transition that this should match?
  2. For Integration, which automation touchpoints should be named first - gas-line checks, leak checks, or something already in flight on the floor?
  3. What is the right cadence for the snapshot - quarterly with the TRL review, or monthly as pilots begin?
  4. Which single process should be the first "pilot with a bar", so Validation has a real line to clear?
Why no numbers hereA snapshot that leadership trusts has one source per number. The measured dimensions already have their pages (the scorecard, analytics, the lab); linking to them keeps one truth. The unmeasured ones are drawn as horizon so the picture is complete without being inflated.
Why glasses are one laneNot every work instruction belongs on a face. Some belong to a robot; some are best on an iPad with a sharper verdict. Measuring readiness along the glasses lane alone would reward the wrong thing.
What stays true to todayEverything in the middle column ships and is read on a live page. The scorecard's Glasses transition tab is the Validation row made concrete for one lane; this framework is the frame around it.
