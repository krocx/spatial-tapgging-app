Life of a work instruction

from its source of record to a signed-off run - the sources as lanes, the stages as a strip, one row for what can go wrong

Proprietary & Confidential · Applied Materials

A work instruction is born somewhere else. SIB's job is to take it from its source, make it spatial, deliver it to whatever the person is holding or wearing, and keep the proof. Today the sources are the formats the proof-of-concept exercised; the destination is live pull from the source of record, converted and adapted to AR with no publish-and-import loop. The stages after import are the same for every lane, which is the point.

Why the lanes convergeIf every source produced its own kind of guide, every device would need every converter. One import step means the Studio, the readiness matrix, the runtimes and the scorecard never know or care where a guide came from. Adding a source is one lane, not eight changes.
Why Cortona is one laneIt exercised the hardest case - a full 3D publication with hidden parts, exploded starts and callouts - and that is exactly why the import step is under review now. It was a proof-of-concept task. It does not scale across the organisation and was never meant to be the spine.
What live pull changesNothing after stage 1. The source of record is read when the shift starts, converted and adapted on the way in; shaping, placing, readiness, running and evidence stay as they are. That is why the stages are drawn as one strip for every lane.
