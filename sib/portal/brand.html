Brand guidelines for the Connected Worker AR OMS Platform

How SIB looks, sounds and moves — on the portal, the catalogue, the platform story, the XR kit and the iPad. One system, written down once, so every surface we ship feels like the same place, and so people who see it want to come back.

01 · PRINCIPLES

Three ideas behind everything

SIB lives in a cleanroom and puts instructions on real equipment. Its look comes from that — not from a dashboard template. If a design choice can't be traced to one of these three, it doesn't belong.

Cleanroom

Light paper, hairline rules, near-square corners. No glass, no gradients, no shadows. Calm surfaces let the equipment, the model and the data be the colour.

Register

Things don't fade in — they lock in. The registration mark (QR finder square, wafer alignment cross, AR anchor) is our motif: focus, loading and section markers all use it.

Honest colour

Blue is the mark and the action. Green means verified. Red is reserved for real failure. There is no orange in the system; the two product colours users already know — Gemba, iLOTO — stay exactly as they are.

02 · THE WORDMARK

appliedx — always lowercase, always two colours

The name is one word: applied in Focus blue, x in Warm green, in the brand face at regular weight. It is written this way in decks, on the portal, in the app and in prose. Never "AppliedX", never one colour, never a different face.

03 · MARK & MOTION

The registration mark, and the one way things move

Four brackets closing on a target. It is what an operator sees on the printed QR, what a tool sees on a wafer, and what the app draws when it anchors. It is the focus ring, the loading state, the section marker and the dot in the wordmark.

Three colours only.
Blue for focus and navigation, green for verified and anchored, ink for structure.
Lock-in.
Elements register: scale 1.35 → 1 in 220 ms on the brand ease. Nothing fades or slides. Loading is the mark locking in. Reduced-motion collapses it to 1 ms.

07 · THE AR SURFACE

10 · RULES

Self-enforcing, so it stays ours

The system is a set of files, not a memory. npm run brand:check fails any governed page that drifts, and the doctrine in the repo tells anyone — or any assistant — generating a page here how it must look.

Always
  • Colours, sizes and durations from the tokens only.
  • Icons from the sprite.
  • The mark for focus, loading and section markers; lock-in for arrival.
  • Arial for reading, the monospace for identifiers and numbers.
  • Product colour on chips, cards and diagram bars — the same colour on the map, the portal and the app.
  • Hairlines for elevation; 2-px corners.
Never
  • Glass blur, gradients on surfaces, drop shadows.
  • Emoji as icons. Rounded 12-px cards. Pill chips.
  • Orange, amber or yellow anywhere but the Gemba product mark.
  • Focus blue or Warm green as text.
  • Navy dashboards, electric-blue accents, system-blue links.
  • A page-level palette or font — if it is not in brand/, it does not exist.
