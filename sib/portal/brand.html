Brand guidelines for the Connected Worker AR OMS Platform

The iPad app is the reference. Everything SIB shows on the web — the portal, the catalogue, the platform story, the XR kit — wears the app's colours, corners and type, so a person who knows the app already knows SIB. This page is that agreement, written down and enforced.

01 · PRINCIPLES

Three ideas behind everything

The app already has a look people like. The system's job is to carry it to the web exactly, and to add the few signatures that make a SIB page unmistakably ours.

The app is the reference

Charcoal surfaces, 8 %-white cards with an accent stroke, white text in opacity tiers, iOS accents, 18-pt corners, capsule chips. If the app does it, SIB does it. If the app doesn't, SIB doesn't invent it.

Register

Our signatures: the appliedx wordmark on every surface, the registration mark for focus and loading, the lock-in motion, the green rim-light in AR. Those are what a template can't copy.

Honest colour

Colours mean the same thing everywhere: blue acts, green is verified or AR OMS, orange is attention or Gemba, red is failure or iLOTO. A colour is never decoration.

03 · THE WORDMARK

appliedx — always lowercase, always two colours

One word. applied in the wordmark blue, x in the wordmark green, regular weight — as the team writes it in decks, on the portal, in the app, in prose. Never "AppliedX", never one colour, never a different face. These two colours belong to the wordmark alone.

05 · MARK & MOTION

The registration mark, and the one way things move

Four brackets closing on a target: what an operator sees on the printed QR, what a tool sees on a wafer, what the app draws when it anchors. It is the focus ring, the loading state, the section marker and the dot in the wordmark. It is the one thing on a SIB page that no template has.

Three colours only.
Blue for focus and navigation, green for verified and anchored, white for structure.
Lock-in.
Elements register: scale 1.35 → 1 in 220 ms on the brand ease. Nothing fades or slides. Loading is the mark locking in. Reduced-motion collapses it to 1 ms.

11 · RULES

Self-enforcing, so it stays ours

The system is a set of files, not a memory. npm run brand:check fails any governed page that drifts, and the doctrine in the repo tells anyone — or any assistant — generating a page here how it must look.

Always
  • Colours, sizes and durations from the tokens only — the app's values.
  • Icons from the sprite.
  • The wordmark component; the mark for focus, loading and section markers; lock-in for arrival.
  • Arial for reading, the monospace for identifiers and numbers.
  • Product colour on doors, chips, cards and diagram bars — the same colour as the app's door.
  • 18-pt cards, 14-pt CTAs, 12-pt fields, capsule chips.
Never
  • Glass blur or drop shadows — a stroke marks a card, as in the app.
  • Emoji as icons.
  • A colour without a meaning. A new hue the app doesn't have.
  • "AppliedX", a one-colour wordmark, or the wordmark colours used anywhere else.
  • A page-level palette or font — if it is not in brand/, it does not exist.
  • Internal codes in anything a user reads.
