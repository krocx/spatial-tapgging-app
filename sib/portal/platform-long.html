Why we're doing this

Ready for the autonomous cleanroom — starting with the technician

Tools and robots will take the repetitive work. The technician's job becomes supervising, intervening and validating — and that job needs spatial context delivered in the moment. We build that muscle now, and bank the wins along the way.

Today
Do-it-right-the-first-time, on every shift. The instruction sits on the equipment, the 3D ghost shows the correct result, and the platform confirms the step before the technician moves on. Skipped steps and tribal knowledge stop being the failure mode.
Smarter
Technicians work smarter, not harder. The guide teaches while they work (AR OJT), adapts to what is in front of them, and captures evidence without a clipboard. New hires reach competency in days; experts stop re-documenting.
Next-gen
The autonomous cleanroom still has a human in the loop. Semi-autonomous today, fully autonomous tomorrow — either way someone has to know where, what and in what state, instantly. Adaptive guidance on the tool is that capability, learned on today's procedures.
Spatial
Spatial intelligence is the durable asset. Every anchor, world map, validation reference and run leaves Applied with a growing spatial understanding of its own tools. That data is device-independent: it powers today's AR on iPads and is exactly what next-generation spatial computing will consume.

What leadership gets

  • One initiative, one number set. Productivity, Velocity and Customer Trust reported per configuration, from the platform's own usage log — not from a survey.
  • A capability Applied owns. Content, spatial data and validation stay on Applied infrastructure; no vendor app between us and our tools.
  • A future-proof entry point. Value on iPads today; the same content and data light up smart glasses as they mature (see Devices below).
  • A path, not a bet. First BU POC → pilot → scale by configuration, with the FY27 device POCs running alongside.

Where Applied can be ahead

  • Own the backbone, not a vendor's app. Every product here runs on SIB — anchors, world maps, validation and usage data are Applied's, deployable on-prem inside the fab network.
  • Validate, don't just display. Most AR tools show steps. Ours checks them spatially, records the verdict, and never blocks the line — a failed check can be overridden, but it is logged.
  • Pluggable intelligence. Validation runs on our engine today and can call any model — internal or third-party — through an API, so we ride every improvement in vision AI without rebuilding the platform.
  • One platform, many BUs. OMS, on-the-job training, EHS audits and lockout/tagout share the same authoring, access control and reporting — each BU gets a product, Applied keeps one system.
  • Authoring measured in minutes. Guides come from Excel, JSON or MES exports, or from the visual Procedure Designer — and any guide can be edited round-trip.

Procedures shown in AR on the actual tool — each step pinned where the work is, with 3D ghosts of the correct result, adapting to the technician and the chamber in front of them.
